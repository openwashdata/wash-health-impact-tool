# Stage 2 report: methodology, factor tables and additionality guidance

Scratch reproduction of phase 2 of the Aqua for All assignment "Developing a water and sanitation - health impact estimation tool for outcome-based finance". Built on 2026-10-23 by an agent for Lars Schöbitz on top of the stage 1 evidence base. Nothing here has been reviewed by a person. Local repository on branch `dev`, no remote.

The decisions taken before the run were applied and not reopened: enterprise-specific baseline times relative risk as the primary route; WHO's PAF route for deaths and DALYs with the direct mortality odds ratios as a sensitivity band; STH as a secondary outcome with the Cochrane 2022 OR 0.86 labelled low confidence; MHH as a reach-only pathway; use, baseline source, follow-up decay and a sanitation coverage threshold as adjustments with reporting bias as a flag; FSM and safely managed on-site sanitation modelled as basic sanitation with the coverage rule and an evidence gap flag in every output; WHO GHO and DHS baselines only (plus the JMP ladder through the openwashdata jmpdata package, which is WHO/UNICEF data); WHO's method re-implemented from the published description; CC BY 4.0 for tables and documents, MIT for code.

Also in the examples: the Tap Effect bathroom connections (679) carried as a quality metric and, had promotion been delivered, as a hygiene pathway; the institutional connections and the Aqua Clara school hubs (8 600 pupils) as reach only; the KWSH case with an improved baseline source (RR 0.97, no measurable effect) and with verified water quality (RR 0.48); three years of filter service with decay (year weight 2.24 instead of 3); the direct mortality band for KWSH (1.3 all-cause under-five deaths averted, 0.5 to 2.0, against 0.12 by the PAF route) and the WHO scenario comparison (0.37 deaths, all ages, against 0.39 by the per-person formula).

The PAF check in the methodology: the attributable fractions re-implemented from the public JMP ladder sit within about 0.1 of WHO's published values for water and sanitation in most of the 11 countries (Cambodia water 0.34 against 0.33; India water 0.14 against 0.30 is the largest gap) and well below WHO's for hygiene (0.05 to 0.27 against 0.24 to 0.29), because WHO adjusts facility presence for the probability of handwashing after faecal contact and the ladder does not. The chain's PAF route uses WHO's published fractions, not the re-implemented ones; the comparison is a check on the re-implementation.

## 3. Where the evidence forced a judgement call

Each is written into the row's `notes` or `use_rule` and flagged in outputs.

1. **Baseline incidence from the DHS two-week prevalence.** No country incidence series exists in the WHO GHO or DHS pulls. Choice: convert the DHS prevalence with the mean episode duration of 4.3 days (Lamberti 2012) using incidence = prevalence x 365 / (14 + duration); the regional modelled incidence (Fischer Walker 2012) is the fallback and sits in the table for comparison. The DHS "five years preceding the survey" rows were chosen over the "three years" rows for comparability across surveys.
2. **Use reference values.** The pooled relative risks embed partial adherence, so a verified use share cannot be applied as a plain multiplier without double counting. Choice: multiplier = min(1, verified use / trial-typical adherence), with the reference taken as the median of the compliance values listed in the Cochrane 2015 trial tables (chlorination 0.62 from 12 trials, filtration 0.90 from 13 LMIC trials). Solar disinfection and sanitation have no reference; the verified share applies directly (conservative). Hygiene has no use multiplier.
3. **Follow-up decay.** Only the filter evidence gives a long-run estimate (RR 0.69 at 12 months or more against 0.50 pooled), so the decay factor 0.62 is derived from those two rows. Choice: apply it as a proxy to chlorination and solar disinfection with a flag; no numeric decay for sanitation (direction known, size not reported) or hygiene; none for connections (active share per year instead).
4. **Sanitation coverage threshold at 50 percent.** The mortality review contrasts community-wide sanitation with household-targeted sanitation "where the majority of the community did not already use improved sanitation"; Larsen 2017 finds a diarrhoea effect only at 100 percent coverage. No study estimates a threshold. Choice: 50 percent, following the word majority; below it the household-targeted rows apply (RR 0.98, OR 1.07) and the label is very low.
5. **Population denominators.** Decision 7 limited baselines to WHO GHO and DHS, which carry no population counts. Choice: derive the 2019 population from SDG 3.9.2 deaths divided by the SDG 3.9.2 rate (both WHO), and the under-five population from the DHS share aged 0 to 4; regional under-five shares from WHO Annex 4.
6. **Under-five relative risks applied to all ages** for deaths and DALYs, as WHO does. Cases are estimated for children under five only because no all-ages incidence baseline exists.
7. **Direct mortality band conversion.** The under-five mortality rate (per 1000 live births) is turned into an annual rate per child-year with the factor 0.2; odds ratios are treated as relative risks at the low annual death risk.
8. **Low estimates floored at zero** where the effect's confidence interval includes no effect (water supply, basic sanitation), with a note. The alternative (negative cases averted) is statistically honest but would mislead a funder's table.
9. **Uncertainty propagation limited to the effect interval.** The PAF, the counterfactual RR, the incidence and the household size enter as point values. Stated in the limitations.
10. **Absolute service-ladder relative risks** for the scenario comparison: limited and basic off premises at 0.81, on premises 0.79, safely managed water 0.48, safely managed sanitation mapped to the sewered value 0.53 (overstates for on-site systems; flagged), basic hygiene facility 0.70 without WHO's practice adjustment.
11. **Reach-only pathways.** Institutional connections, emptying services for existing toilets, MHH, and hygiene products without a promotion contact return people reached and the label "not estimable" rather than a borrowed factor.
12. **Pathway proxies with evidence gap flags.** Container-based or on-site toilets with a service use the basic sanitation row; in-line chlorination uses the household chlorination row; kiosks use the improved-source-off-premises row.
13. **Stage 1 file corrected.** The stage 1 GHO extract had merged the all-ages and under-five burden rows (identical dimension strings) and kept only national coverage rows; the stage 2 rebuild keeps the age and residence dimensions. The DHS rows now carry the by-variable label so that the right total is picked.

## 4. What could not be done

- No STH baseline prevalence for any country (WHO PCT databank and ESPEN not pulled); STH infections averted are computed only when the user supplies a prevalence.
- No pooled effect exists for FSM services, safely managed on-site sanitation, water kiosks or in-line chlorination; proxies with flags, or reach only.
- No all-ages diarrhoea incidence baseline; cases are for children under five.
- Sanitation and hygiene ladders are not in jmpdata 0.1.0 for these countries; the GHO series were used instead, which cannot separate limited from unimproved sanitation.
- Safely managed water is missing for Kenya and Rwanda and safely managed sanitation for Rwanda (no JMP estimate).
- The Kremer et al. February 2026 revision (18 trials, OR 0.76) was not read; the row carries the 2023 value with a note.
- No subnational baselines; the DHS API offers them but they were not pulled.
- The Wolf 2022 sanitation follow-up coefficient is not reported numerically in the paper, so sanitation decay stays unquantified.
- No valuation step (cost per DALY, benefit-cost ratios); the economic rows from stage 1 are not wired in.
- Uganda's diarrhoea prevalence and South Africa's DHS values are from 2016.
- No human review of any table row, test or document.

## 5. Open questions for stage 3

1. **Tool format.** The chain is a plain R function set over CSV tables. Options: an R package (`whit` or similar, tables as package data with a version tag and a `factors_version()` function), a Quarto report template that takes an input CSV of outputs and renders the tables of the worked examples, and a small Shiny app for ex-ante what-if runs. All three can share the same package.
2. **Shiny scope.** Minimum: one form per output (pathway, units, country, setting, baseline source, verified use, years, coverage, water quality), results table with low, central, high and label, ledger download. Should the app also hold the baseline trajectory and compute the attributable part, or stay a calculator?
3. **Flat export for Airtable or mWater.** The factor tables are already flat. A one-row-per-pathway "factor card" export (effect RR with interval, use reference, decay, PAF normalisation per country) would let Airtable or mWater formulas reproduce the central estimate; the label logic and the ledger would stay in R. Which platform does Aqua for All run, and is a formula-level reproduction wanted or only an import of results?
4. **Input schema.** Agree the input fields with the verifier's report format (units by year, use share, baseline source at sign-up, water quality results, coverage survey) so ex-post runs need no re-keying.
5. **Factor table governance.** Who approves a new row, how often the WHO, JMP and DHS pulls rerun, and whether the tag is `factors-vYYYY.MM`.
6. **Validation cases.** Replace the illustrative counts (Aqua Clara filters, ATEC toilets, Tap Effect institutions) with transaction data from Aqua for All and Roots of Impact, and add one hygiene and one MHH enterprise.
7. **STH and valuation.** Whether to pull ESPEN school-age STH prevalence by country and whether to add a cost per DALY step from the stage 1 economic rows.
8. **Communication format.** Whether the funder wants deaths and DALYs at transaction level at all (they are fractions per year) or only at portfolio level, as the guidance recommends.
